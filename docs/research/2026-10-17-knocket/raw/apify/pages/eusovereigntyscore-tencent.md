Tencent Cloud is the cloud computing service of the Chinese technology group Tencent. Its portfolio covers virtual machines, GPU servers, containers and Kubernetes, object and block storage, managed databases, content delivery, DNS and security services, as well as AI services such as speech recognition, machine translation and a large language model service. It is delivered as a public cloud from data centres in Asia, Europe and North America.

Tencent Cloud is part of Tencent, a multinational technology company headquartered in Shenzhen, China, and is therefore subject to Chinese law. The website lists a Frankfurt, Germany region, which allows some services to be hosted in the EU. It does not describe data residency commitments, a GDPR data processing agreement or the contracting entity for European customers. Before storing personal data, a European organisation should check the chosen region, the contract and the rules for government access requests.

## Why digital sovereignty matters with Tencent Cloud

Tencent Cloud is headquartered in CN, outside the EU. Contracts and data therefore also fall under non-European law. The GDPR applies through contractual terms, but in a conflict with local legislation you have less certainty than with a European vendor.

Tencent Cloud hosts data in EU, OTHER. Exactly what stays within the EU — and what does not — is defined by the vendor's data processing agreement and sub-processor list, not by a setting you control yourself; check those documents for the precise scope, including backups, logs and support access.

Tencent Cloud is closed source and hosted by the vendor. The encryption keys and the runtime sit with the vendor and switching requires data migration. Ask about bring-your-own-key, export options and open standards.
